Open gaps, crumbling pointing and weeds in joints can make a patio difficult to keep tidy. Ant’s Jets can assess repointing enquiries in Godalming; whether joint renewal is the right work depends on the actual paving, not the location alone.

Separate a joint problem from a water problem

If water sits on your patio, send a photo taken after rain as well as a dry close-up of the joints. A fresh joint finish cannot change the direction in which slabs fall. Where a paved route meets a rear door, show the threshold and any channel drain, so the proposed work can account for those edges without blocking drainage.

  • Photograph puddles before they dry, noting how long they remain.
  • Show where paving meets the door and any drainage channel.
  • Tell us whether access is through a side passage or a shared route.

How the work is assessed and planned

Assess the cause

Start with wide photos, joint close-ups, your postcode and access details. Slab movement, pooling water and damaged bedding need assessment before joint replacement is agreed.

Prepare sound joints

Where repointing is suitable, failed filling needs appropriate removal without damaging slab edges. Clean the surface and joints using a method suited to the paving, keep outlets clear and check the required joint depth.

Choose and apply a compatible system

Agree the material after checking the paving, bedding and joint sizes. Follow that product’s preparation and application requirements rather than choosing on colour alone.

Protect the finish

Weather, drying and curing requirements determine the work sequence. Keep people and pets off for the advised period and follow the agreed aftercare before washing or moving furniture back.

Which jointing material is suitable?

There is no single material that suits every patio. The options below are subject to inspection; they are not a specification for your paving.

Sand and cement / mortar-based pointing

A compatible sand-and-cement or proprietary mortar system can give a firm traditional joint on suitable rigidly laid paving. It needs careful mixing, depth and finishing; a rigid joint can crack if the slabs move, and careless application can mark porous stone. A thin skim over failed joints is not a repair.

Resin and brush-in jointing compounds

Some resin-based compounds are brushed into prepared joints, which can make application practical on suitable paving. Brush-in describes the method, not one universal chemistry: products vary in drainage, bedding, width, depth and weather requirements. Poor compatibility or preparation can cause failure, and residue can mark some surfaces. The actual system is agreed after inspection.

Porcelain and block paving

Porcelain may require a compatible exterior grout with suitable joint widths and careful residue removal. Conventional flexible block paving generally needs the specified jointing sand instead of rigid slab pointing. These are not interchangeable treatments.

Questions about patio repointing in Godalming

Will repointing stop puddles on my Godalming patio?

Not if the cause is the paving level, a blocked outlet or a bedding problem. Photos of the wet patio and its drains help identify whether joint replacement is appropriate or whether drainage needs attention first.

Will repointing fix loose slabs?

No. Joint replacement does not repair unstable bedding or reset paving levels. Moving, sunken or rocking slabs need assessment first; the scope of any separate repair must be agreed before repointing.

Can you choose the material from a photo?

Photos help with initial advice, but joint dimensions, bedding, drainage and the paving material may need an inspection. No particular brand or system is promised before suitability is checked.

Can cleaning and repointing be arranged together?

Yes, they can be assessed as a combined job. The surface, existing joints, weather and product requirements determine the sequence, which may need more than one visit.
